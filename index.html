ILLUSTRATIVE CASE 01 · Customer operations

An enquiry desk that gives people an answer—and the team some breathing room.

Fictional scenario: a regional home-services business handling booking enquiries across several locations.

⇄

The challenge

Calls, website forms, and messages arrive separately. Staff re-enter details, customers repeat themselves, and managers cannot see which enquiries need attention.

The proposed solution

Map common enquiry types and existing handoffs. Pilot an assisted voice and chat flow for routine questions, connect enquiry capture to a CRM, and route booking exceptions to a person with the conversation context.

What we would build

  • Enquiry map and call flows
  • CRM integration and routing rules
  • Approved knowledge base and escalation paths
  • Pilot dashboard and team training

How we would roll it out

Start with one location and a small set of approved questions. Compare the pilot with a recorded baseline; review missed escalations and customer feedback before expanding.

Response timeTime to first useful answer
Routing qualityRequests reaching the right team
Team workloadManual touches per enquiry
What would success look like?

For this scenario, measure repeat contacts, routing errors, and manual handling time. Any future before-and-after figures must come from the client’s actual pilot.

ILLUSTRATIVE CASE 02 · Content & marketing

From a blank page every Monday to a content workflow the team can repeat.

Fictional scenario: an education brand producing course campaigns, social posts, and email content.

✎

The challenge

Subject experts, writers, and designers work from scattered documents. Feedback arrives late, messaging varies by channel, and the team spends too much time coordinating drafts.

The proposed solution

Create a shared messaging framework and content calendar. Use structured briefs and AI-assisted first drafts, with subject-expert review, brand checks, and approval before publishing. Feed campaign learning back into the next brief.

What we would build

  • Messaging framework and reusable briefs
  • Drafting and review workflow
  • Publishing calendar and asset library
  • Campaign measurement template

How we would roll it out

Pilot one campaign theme across a small set of channels. Keep review ownership explicit, check factual accuracy, and compare production effort with the current workflow.

Cycle timeBrief to approved asset
QualityReview rounds and corrections
ConsistencyAssets using approved messaging
What would success look like?

Track drafting time separately from review time. Higher output alone is not success; check usefulness, accuracy, and the campaign’s agreed business measures.

ILLUSTRATIVE CASE 03 · Sales & growth

A lead journey that keeps the conversation going after the campaign ends.

Fictional scenario: a B2B services company generating leads through paid campaigns and referrals.

↗

The challenge

Leads sit in spreadsheets, assignment depends on someone noticing a notification, and sales cannot tell which campaigns create worthwhile conversations.

The proposed solution

Clarify the offer and qualification criteria. Connect landing-page forms to a CRM, assign ownership, introduce appropriate follow-up reminders, and build a source-to-opportunity view for marketing and sales.

What we would build

  • Offer and landing-page recommendations
  • Lead capture and CRM pipeline
  • Assignment and follow-up rules
  • Shared funnel dashboard

How we would roll it out

Launch with one offer and one sales team. Validate lead routing, review the first conversations, and adjust messaging and follow-up based on real objections.

Follow-upLead to first contact
Lead qualityQualified conversations by source
VisibilityLeads with an owner and next action
What would success look like?

Establish a baseline for contact time, qualified lead rate, and stage progression. Agree what counts as qualified before interpreting any conversion change.

A few useful answers

Before we
get started.

Have a problem that doesn’t fit one of our examples? That’s a good reason to start a conversation.

Are you a marketing agency, a consultancy, or a technology company?

Elephant in Orbit brings those capabilities together around a business problem. The engagement may involve strategy, marketing, technology, process changes, or a combination. We define the work around what needs to improve.

Can we come to you without a clear brief?

Yes. Bring the situation as you understand it: what happens today, what is frustrating your team, and what you would like to improve. Discovery helps turn that into a concrete problem and a practical next step.

Do you always recommend AI or automation?

We choose the approach after understanding the problem. Some challenges need automation; others need a simpler process, clearer ownership, better messaging, or training. Where AI is useful, we define the review and escalation steps it needs.

Will you work with our current tools and internal team?

We begin by understanding your existing systems and capabilities. We assess what can be retained, what needs connecting, and what might need replacing. Required access, licenses, internal owners, and third-party dependencies are agreed during scoping.

What happens after launch?

The scope defines testing, handover, documentation, and any support period. Ongoing monitoring, optimization, or additional delivery can be arranged as a separate partnership.

Can you guarantee a specific increase in revenue or cost savings?

We agree on intended outcomes and useful measures, but business results depend on the starting point, execution, adoption, and market conditions. We establish a baseline and evaluate progress against the agreed scope.

Are the case studies on this page real client projects?

The current case studies are clearly labeled fictional scenarios. They illustrate our proposed approach and deliverables. They do not represent completed engagements or verified performance results.

How do pricing and timelines work?

We scope the challenge, dependencies, deliverables, and required involvement first. You receive an agreed proposal before work begins. Published prices and standard turnaround times will be added when the service packages are finalized.
